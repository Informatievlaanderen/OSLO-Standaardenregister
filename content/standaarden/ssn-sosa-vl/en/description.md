<p>The <strong>SSN SOSA VL model</strong> (Flemish Standard for sensor, observation, sampling and actuator) 
    provides a standardised framework for managing and exchanging information 
    <strong>sensor, observation, sampling and actuator data</strong> in Flanders.
    The focus is on the effective representation and application of international standards within 
    the theme of <strong>measurements, monitoring, sampling and automation</strong>.
    The model closely aligns with the <strong>W3C/OGC model SSN SOSA</strong> and is aligned with 
    complementary models such as <strong>PROV-O</strong>, <strong>QUDT</strong>, and where relevant 
    <strong>p-plan</strong> and <strong>GeoSPARQL</strong>.</p>

<p>Within the framework, different <strong>types of entities</strong> are classified that are relevant for:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Sensors</li>
    <li>Observations</li>
    <li>Sampling</li>
    <li>actuators</li>
</ul>
<p>In addition, the data examples facilitate the integration of 
    <strong>semantic and procedural references</strong>, including design principles, 
    best practices and agreements on modelling. 
    This results in an unambiguous and well-documented standard for recording 
    data across a variety of domains.</p>

<p>The framework is the central point of reference for the description and structuring of:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Observations</li>
    <li>Measurements</li>
    <li>Sampling</li>
    <li>Systems</li>
    <li>Procedures</li>
    <li>actuators</li>
</ul>

<p>It supports different types of data sources, such as:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Sensor networks</li>
    <li>Field measurements</li>
    <li>Laboratory analyses</li>
    <li>Automated measuring systems</li>
</ul>

<p>Within this framework, various model elements are distinguished, including 
    <strong>Observations, samples, sensors, actuators</strong> and <strong>procedures</strong>. 
    These elements allow for a uniform and interpretable data structure.</p>

<p>The model makes it possible to analyse relationships between observations and their context in detail. 
    Through links with <strong>PROV-O</strong> and <strong>QUDT</strong> , among others, 
    <strong>procedures, units, uncertainties and uncertainties</strong> used 
    <strong>quality aspects</strong> are accurately recorded.
    This is essential for assessing reliability, reproducibility and comparability 
    of measurement data.</p>

<p>Furthermore, the framework provides a clear way to 
    <strong>documenting evolution, execution and procedural steps</strong> around observations and sampling.
    Key stages such as:</p>
<ul>
    <li>data collection</li>
    <li>Validation</li>
    <li>Quality Control</li>
    <li>Processing</li>
</ul>
<p>can be formally described. 
    This ensures transparent and complete documentation of methods and processes used.</p>
